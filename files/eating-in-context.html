Jooyun Ha · Working papers, 2016–2025

Eating problems in the context of life

For eight years I have returned to one question: why do people eat more than they intend to, even when they know better? These are the actual working documents, in order. Each revision came from a different seat: cognitive psychology, clinical nutrition, and then my own company.

Scroll sideways to follow the trail →
2016

Mapping what the literature already knew

MA in Cognitive Psychology, Sungkyunkwan University · Advisor: Prof. Do Kyung Soo

I reviewed determinant models of eating behavior and redrew six categories of determinants as my own cause-and-effect diagrams. Two notes I wrote on the slides stayed with me: more income does not mean a healthier diet, and better access does not mean a different choice.

Sources reviewed: Verstraeten et al. (2014); Deliens et al. (2014); Sleddens et al. (2015a, 2015b); Ajzen’s Theory of Planned Behavior; Köster (2009); Gillespie & Johnson-Askew (2009); the Prototype Willingness Model; Rangel on decision-making circuitry.

2017 → 2021 → 2024

A 3×3 of my own: where the problem comes from, and how it acts

Sungkyunkwan University · Clinical Nutrition, Hanyang University · nutricious studio & lab.

The models I had read sorted determinants into the individual and the environment. I added the food stimulus itself as a third source, then crossed the three sources with three mechanisms: cognition, motivation and behavior.
